# 01 — Vision & Pillars

## Logline

A sun-soaked, storm-haunted open-world crime saga about three desperate people
trying to out-hustle a sinking city before it — and the people who own it —
takes them down.

## The feeling we are selling

The fantasy is **velocity with consequence**. The thrill of a coastline at
golden hour with the top down and a trunk full of someone else's money — and
the dread of knowing the tide, the cops, and the cartel are all coming in. We
want a player to feel like the smartest, fastest, most dangerous person in the
room one minute, and a small animal in a very large storm the next.

## Design pillars

These five pillars are the tie-breakers. When two good ideas conflict, the one
that serves a higher pillar wins.

### 1. The City Is the Star
Port Soleil is not a backdrop; it is a character with moods, rhythms, and a
memory. Districts feel economically and culturally distinct. NPCs have routines.
Weather and tide reshape what's possible. If a feature doesn't make the city
feel more alive, it's suspect.

### 2. Freedom With Friction
Total sandbox freedom, but the world pushes back believably. Steal a car and its
owner calls it in. Shoot in a quiet neighborhood and witnesses scatter and dial.
Consequence should feel *fair and legible*, never random. The player should
always be able to point at *why* it went sideways.

### 3. Three Lives, One Story
Switchable protagonists aren't a gimmick — they're a storytelling and tactical
engine. Each lead opens parts of the world (and the player's heart) the others
can't. Switching mid-heist is the signature verb.

### 4. Crime Is a Craft, Not a Cutscene
Heists and scores are *planned, equipped, and improvised*, not watched. Player
choice in approach (loud, quiet, clever) is mechanically real and economically
weighted. Botching a plan should create the *best* stories.

### 5. Tone: Sincere Pulp
Satire and heart, not nihilism. The world is absurd and we mock it, but the
*people* are real and we love them. The comedy is in the institutions; the
tragedy is in the characters. Never punch down.

## Anti-pillars (things we refuse to be)

- **Not a checklist museum.** Map icons are invitations, not chores. Quality of
  encounters over quantity of dots.
- **Not grind-gated.** Money and progression respect the player's time. No
  systems exist primarily to sell shortcuts.
- **Not cruelty for shock.** Edge is earned through character, not gore-bait or
  bigotry.
- **Not a derivative.** Every name, brand, place, and face is ours. See doc 12.

## Target experience metrics

| Question we ask in playtest | Target answer |
|---|---|
| "Did the city feel alive?" | 90%+ yes |
| "Did you understand why you got caught/killed?" | 85%+ yes |
| "Did you make a story you'd retell a friend?" | Every 2-hour session |
| "Did you care about the three leads?" | By end of Act 1 |
| "Did you feel forced to spend money to keep up?" | <5% yes |

## Platform & scope intent

- **Primary:** current-gen consoles + high-end PC, single-player campaign first.
- **Live shared world** ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)) as a parallel,
  not a tax on the campaign.
- **Accessibility is a launch pillar,** not a patch: full remapping, scalable
  difficulty per-system (driving, combat, stealth), colorblind-safe HUD, aim and
  traversal assists, content warnings with skip/soften options.
