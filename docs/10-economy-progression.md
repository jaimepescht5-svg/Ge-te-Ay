# 10 — Economy & Progression

## Design stance

Progression respects the player's **time and intelligence**. Money is earned
through play, not pain; there are **no systems built to sell shortcuts**. Power
grows along *multiple* axes so players build the criminal they want to be.

## Money: three kinds

1. **Dirty cash** — hot money straight from a job. Spendable on the street and the
   gray market, but flagged: carrying or banking too much raises suspicion.
2. **Clean money** — laundered funds, safe to invest in legit assets, property,
   and high-end purchases.
3. **Crew funds** — a shared pool for heist prep and crew pay, separate from each
   lead's personal wealth.

### Laundering (a real subsystem)
Turning dirty cash clean is a gameplay loop, not a menu click:
- **Front businesses** (a car wash, a club, the garage, a supper club) launder a
  capped amount per cycle — buy and upgrade them to clean more, faster.
- **Risk:** over-launder and you draw an audit (a mission/Heat event). Balance
  greed against exposure.

## Per-character progression

Each lead grows independently across shared and signature trees:

- **Shared skills:** Strength, Stamina, Shooting, Stealth, Driving, Lung capacity,
  etc. — improved by *use* (drive well, get better at driving) plus trainers.
- **Signature trees:**
  - **Rae → Redline / Wheelcraft:** longer focus, stunt mastery, vehicle control,
    pursuit evasion.
  - **Theo → Overwatch / Systems:** more tags, longer wallhack, drone/camera
    control, faster hacks, gadget unlocks.
  - **Frankie → Hot Streak / Hustle:** melee finishers, fast-talk options, street
    contacts, better fence prices.
- **Use-based growth** keeps progression diegetic; **trainers and side stories**
  unlock the high tiers.

## Assets & businesses

The empire layer — turning scores into steady income and utility:

| Asset | Income | Utility |
|---|---|---|
| **Tomás's Garage (HQ)** | — | Vehicle storage, mods, crew planning hub |
| **Car wash / club / supper club** | Passive + laundering | Cash cleaning, safehouse |
| **Chop shop** | Per-vehicle payouts | Steal-to-order job board |
| **Smuggling routes (Keys/Glades)** | High, risky | Contraband economy |
| **Vending/parking/laundromat** fronts | Small, steady | Map-wide cash drip |
| **Properties/safehouses** | — | Fast travel, storage, Heat cooldown |

Businesses can be **attacked, audited, or shut down** by rivals and cops —
ownership is active, not passive. Defending them spawns missions.

## Spending sinks (worth the money)

- **Vehicles & customization** ([doc 08](08-vehicles.md))
- **Weapons, gear, gadgets, disguises** ([doc 09](09-weapons-combat.md))
- **Property & business acquisition/upgrades**
- **Crew & specialists** (better heist hires)
- **Lifestyle** (clothing, grooming, hobbies, status) — cosmetic, characterful
- **Bribes & favors** (cut Heat, buy intel, grease a deal)

## Faction reputation (parallel currency)

Standing with **the Vegas, Crane's org, the police, and street crews** is its own
progression. Reputation:
- **Opens/closes content** (jobs, shops, safe passage, ambushes).
- **Shifts prices** (a friendly fence pays more; a hostile district charges a
  "tax").
- **Feeds the ending** ([doc 04](04-story.md)) — who you served changes how it ends.
Rep is a **web of tradeoffs**: pleasing one faction angers another. There is no
"max everything."

## Anti-grind & fairness guarantees

- **Heist payouts scale** so the main path always funds the next chapter — you
  never *have* to grind to progress.
- **Failure is tuition, not bankruptcy:** losing a job costs prep and Heat, never
  your whole net worth.
- **No pay-to-win, ever** (single-player has no purchases; see
  [doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md) for the online monetization stance).
- **Catch-up, not run-away:** systems gently help the broke and tax the hoarders,
  keeping the economy meaningful all game.
