# 45 — Solano Online (Deep Dive)

> Expands the online stance ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)) into a real design:
> the player crew, progression, the co-op heist loop, the business empire,
> anti-grief structure, and how seasons keep the city living
> ([doc 30](30-post-launch-roadmap.md)) — all under the **hard monetization
> charter** ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)). The campaign is never taxed for
> this; online is a parallel home.

## What you make in online: *your* outlaw

- A **created character** (not the three campaign leads — those are story-canon),
  customized within the city's identity ([doc 29](29-wardrobe-and-customization.md)).
- You rise from a nobody freelancer to a crew boss with businesses, a fleet, and a
  reputation — the campaign's fantasy, authored by *you*.

## The player crew (the social core)

- **Form or join a crew** (player org): shared **clubhouse/HQ**, pooled funds,
  roster, and roles echoing the leads — **Wheel / Key / Mouth / Gun**
  ([doc 25](25-glossary-slang.md)).
- **Roles aren't classes** — they're loadout/skill emphases; anyone can drive,
  hack, or fight, but specializing makes co-op heists sing
  ([doc 42](42-signature-abilities.md)).
- **Crew progression** (cosmetic + utility, never power-for-pay): clubhouse
  upgrades, shared businesses, crew identity (colors, emblem, name) — *status,
  not advantage* ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).

## The co-op heist loop (the marquee)

The four-phase system ([doc 07](07-missions.md)) built for **real humans in each
role** — communication and trust *are* the mechanic:

1. **Setup** — a crew member scouts (the Key, Overwatch-style), buys intel, or
   discovers a score.
2. **Planning** — pick Quiet/Loud/Clever, assign roles, split the budget and the
   cut *up front* (a social negotiation the UI supports).
3. **Execution** — parallel tasks for parallel players (driver staging, hacker on
   cameras, gunners on the floor) — the human version of the switch.
4. **Escape & split** — beat the Heat together; the cut pays out per the plan; one
   member's mistake costs the whole crew (real stakes, real trust).

> **Matchmaking for the lonely:** solo/duo players can fill a crew with skilled
> NPC specialists ([doc 10](10-economy-progression.md)) so online heists are
> playable without a full squad — never a "need 4 friends" wall.

## The business empire (offline-fair income)

- Own and run **fronts, smuggling routes, chop shops** that generate **capped, fair
  income** while you're away ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)) — never enough to
  pressure purchases, always enough to feel like progress.
- Businesses can be **contested** (opt-in, structured) — crew wars over territory,
  not random gank-the-AFK-guy ([doc 19](19-living-world-ai.md)).

## PvP — consent-forward (anti-grief in detail)

The genre's biggest online failure is unchecked griefing. Our structure:

- **Default is cooperative.** Open-world is peaceful-leaning; **conflict is opted
  into** via contested zones, declared crew wars, and event arenas
  ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).
- **No reward for ganking randoms** — bounties/scoring only flow from *consensual*
  conflict; spawn-camping pays nothing and flags bad-sport status.
- **Passive mode** and **intent-based matchmaking** (PvE-leaning vs. PvP-leaning
  lobbies) keep players among the like-minded.
- **Skill, not gear, decides fights** — no purchasable power, period
  ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).
- **Strong moderation & reporting**, voice/text filters, robust block/mute
  ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).

## Living-world events (online flavor of the calendar)

The single-player calendar ([doc 38](38-dynamic-events-calendar.md)) returns as
**shared events** that pull the server together:

- **Storm events** (co-op rescue & looting), **festival heists**, **faction-war
  flashpoints**, **regatta/boat-week**, **development booms** — cooperative or
  consensually competitive, never forced griefing ([doc 30](30-post-launch-roadmap.md)).

## Seasons & continuity

- **Free-core seasonal drops** add story, heists, vehicles, and world events; the
  city's continuity **moves forward** (a district floods, a faction gains ground)
  so returning players find a changed Port Soleil
  ([doc 30](30-post-launch-roadmap.md)).
- **Earnable-first rewards**; the only store is **cosmetics**, always also
  earnable, never power ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).

## Technical & fairness guardrails

- **Small instanced sessions** federated into a larger persistent economy — you
  know who you're playing with ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)).
- **First-class anti-cheat & fair-play**, **cross-progression where feasible**, and
  **separate balance/economy** from single-player so neither warps the other.
- **Account & parental controls**, spending limits, and full accessibility parity
  ([doc 23](23-accessibility.md)).

## The online promise (one line)

> **Build your own crew and your own legend in a living Port Soleil — where the
> only things you can buy are cosmetics, the only fights are the ones you choose,
> and everything that matters is something you played your way to.**
