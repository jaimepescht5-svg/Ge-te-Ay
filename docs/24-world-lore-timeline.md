# 24 — World Lore & Timeline

> The deep history of Port Soleil and Solano County — the backstory that makes
> the city feel *old* and *doomed* rather than freshly minted for a game. None of
> this needs to be spelled out to players; it's the iceberg under the waterline,
> surfacing through architecture, murals, radio, and side stories. All invented
> ([doc 12](12-legal-distinctiveness.md)).

## The one-sentence history

**A swamp nobody wanted, drained and sold three times over, that got rich on
ports and vice and is now being bought back — at a premium — by the same kind of
people who first sold it, just as the sea comes to collect.**

## Timeline (in-fiction)

### ~1700s — The drowned coast
The Sablon Coast is salt marsh and barrier islands — fished and traveled by
Indigenous peoples and, later, a tangle of colonial powers who all decide the
swamp isn't worth the malaria. The land is defined from the start by **water that
won't stay put.**

### ~1840s–1880s — The first drainage
Speculators drain the first marshes and lay out **Old Soleil** (today's *the Cut*)
along hand-dug canals — the bones of the city. Built by laborers who are then
priced out of it. **The original sin the present rhymes with.**

### ~1900s–1920s — Port & boom
A deepwater port at **Hialto** (*the Reach*) turns the town into a real city.
First land boom; deco hotels rise; the **Vega** family arrives as dockworkers and
quietly starts running the things the port needs run. A hurricane flattens half
of it. They rebuild anyway. **The pattern is set: boom, storm, rebuild, repeat.**

### ~1930s–1950s — Vice & glamour
Prohibition-era smuggling through the Keys makes fortunes and makes the Vegas. The
city becomes a warm-weather playground — casinos, supper clubs, **Sablon FM**'s
golden age. The glamour is real; so is the rot underneath it.

### ~1960s–1980s — Concrete & cartels
The **Causeways** and the **Interstate loop** are built; **Marisol Heights** is
graded as the dry high ground for the new money; **Sabal Springs** sprawls inland.
The drug trade reroutes through the Keys and the Glades, and the Vegas graduate
from smugglers to an institution — "the only thing standing between the city and
chaos," as they'll always tell you.

### ~1990s–2000s — The Vega peace
La Señora (**Lourdes Vega**) takes the family legitimate-ish: ports, casinos,
politics, construction. A long, profitable, ruthless "peace." The city coasts on
tourism and denial while the tide marks climb a little higher every year.

### ~2010s — The water gets honest
Sunny-day flooding becomes routine. Insurance abandons whole districts;
**NestEgg** moves in to sell what's left. The Cut starts to sink — literally and
economically. The first **"king tide"** that closes Downtown avenues makes
national news, then is forgotten by national news.

### Present (the game) — The Reclamation
**Sterling Crane** arrives from out of state with more money than the Vegas ever
had and a different idea: don't fight the flood — *own it.* Buy the high ground,
"revitalize" the Cut into oblivion, build **The Ark**, and be the last landlord
standing when the water wins. He and the Vegas slide into a cold war over a city
that's running out of dry land. **Into that crossfire fall three strangers with a
bag of the wrong money.** And out at sea, a depression named **Odette** starts to
turn.

## Recurring historical motifs (so the past echoes)

- **"Drained and sold."** Every generation, someone profits by selling the same
  doomed land to the people who'll lose it. The crew can be the first to break the
  cycle — or just its newest beneficiaries.
- **Boom → storm → rebuild.** The city has always answered catastrophe with
  denial and concrete. Act III asks whether that finally ends.
- **The water always wins, eventually.** The flood line on the map
  ([doc 06](06-map-districts.md)) is the city's whole history in one rising number.

## Where the lore surfaces in-game (not as lectures)

- **Architecture & layers:** deco bones under neon, stilt-houses beside towers,
  tide stains on every wall ([doc 15](15-art-direction.md)).
- **Murals & tide-line shrines** ([doc 18](18-side-stories.md)) tell the people's
  history block by block.
- **Radio & talk shows** ([doc 13](13-radio-music.md)) reminisce, mythologize, and
  argue about the past.
- **Faction backstory** ([doc 03a](03a-cast-relationships.md)) — the Vegas'
  century in this mud is *why* La Señora thinks she owns it.
- **Sunken caches** at low tide — literal buried history to dig up.

## Lore guardrails

- **Fictional throughout** — no real historical events, places, or peoples are
  depicted or appropriated; the melting-pot culture is honored, not caricatured
  ([doc 01](01-vision.md), [doc 12](12-legal-distinctiveness.md)).
- **History serves theme,** not trivia: every era on this timeline exists to make
  the present's flood-and-money pressure feel *inevitable.*
