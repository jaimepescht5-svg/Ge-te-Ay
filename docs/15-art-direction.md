# 15 — Art Direction & Visual Identity

## The look in one line

**Sun-bleached paradise rotting at the waterline** — gorgeous, neon-soaked, and
quietly drowning. Beauty and decay in the same frame, always.

## Visual pillars

1. **Heat you can feel.** Hazy air, heat shimmer, sweat-sheen on skin, sun-faded
   paint, glaring noon light. The climate is a character.
2. **Neon vs. nature.** Hand-made human color (neon, pastels, signage) fighting a
   lush, encroaching natural world (palms, swamp, the ever-present water).
3. **The waterline tells the story.** Tide marks, salt damage, mold, sandbags,
   stilts, and flood-staging are *everywhere* — the city's anxiety made visible.
4. **Honest beauty.** We find beauty in the unglamorous (a strip mall at dusk, a
   flooded street reflecting neon) as much as the postcards (the causeway at
   golden hour).

## Color & light by district

| District | Palette | Light signature |
|---|---|---|
| Downtown Core | Neon magenta/cyan over black glass | Night-forward; reflective, electric |
| The Cut (Old Soleil) | Pastels, terracotta, faded murals | Warm golden-hour; intimate |
| Marisol Heights | Cool greens, marble white, gold | Clean, manicured, slightly sterile |
| The Reach (industrial) | Rust, sodium-orange, container primaries | Harsh halogen, deep shadow |
| Cayo Brava / Keys | Turquoise, sand, white, sunset coral | Blown-out bright; high contrast |
| Bayou Verde / Glades | Deep greens, tannin-brown water, gold | Dappled, humid, mist at dawn/dusk |
| Sabal Springs | Bleached beige, sun-cracked signage | Flat, oppressive midday sun |

## The four lighting "moods" (time of day as mood)

- **Dawn:** mist, fishermen, quiet — the city at its most hopeful.
- **Noon:** harsh, sweaty, exposed — danger has nowhere to hide.
- **Golden hour:** the signature — the screenshot, the cruise, the romance.
- **Night:** neon and id — the city's other self comes out.
- **(Special) Storm light:** sickly green-grey, violent, the world holding its
  breath — the visual key of Act III.

## Character & costume design

- **Silhouette-first** so the three leads read instantly at distance (Rae:
  driver's jacket & boots; Theo: hood & layers, hands-in-pockets; Frankie:
  loud shirts, gold, always slightly too much).
- **Costume tells arc:** wardrobe shifts subtly as each lead rises and frays.
- **A real city's faces:** wide, specific diversity in NPC age, body, ethnicity,
  and style — the melting pot, not a mannequin crowd.

## UI / HUD identity

- **Diegetic-leaning, minimal HUD.** Information when you need it, clean frame
  when you don't (fully customizable for accessibility).
- **Original visual language:** our own typeface, iconography, map style, and
  logo — never a pastiche of a competitor's trade dress ([doc 12](12-legal-distinctiveness.md)).
- **The phone** is the hub UI: missions, contacts, apps (Plumage, Beacon, Vantgo),
  photo mode, in-fiction and satirical.
- **Map style:** a stylized, slightly tourist-brochure aesthetic that turns
  ominous (flood overlays, storm tracks) as the season advances.

## Photo mode

A first-class feature, because the city is built to be photographed:
- Full camera control, filters (period film stocks, neon push), depth of field,
  time-of-day and weather scrub where permitted, posing for the leads.
- Drives organic marketing and rewards the art team's work.

## VFX signatures

- **Water & wet:** puddles, spray, rain sheeting, flood reflections, wakes — water
  is the most-rendered material in the game.
- **The storm:** building cloud walls, debris, surge, wind-bent palms, power-grid
  failures rolling across districts.
- **Redline:** Rae's focus state — chromatic streak, time-smear, heightened color.
- **Heat haze, neon bloom, salt grime** as constant atmospheric seasoning.

## References & influences (mood, not copying)

Inspired by the *aesthetics* of warm-climate noir, sun-drenched crime cinema,
and disaster photography — translated into a wholly original city. We chase a
**feeling**, never a frame from an existing work.
