# 11 — Online & the Shared World

## Stance

The online mode — **"Solano Online"** — is a **parallel experience, not a tax on
the campaign.** The single-player story is complete and uncompromised on its own.
Online is for players who want to *live* in Port Soleil with friends. Crucially:
**no predatory monetization, no pay-to-win, no FOMO treadmills.** (See the
monetization charter below — it's a hard pillar, not marketing.)

## What it is

A persistent, shared Port Soleil where small groups of players build their own
crews, run businesses, pull heists together, and climb the same criminal ladder —
with the single-player city's systems (Heat, factions, tide, storms) intact.

## Core online loops

- **Build a crew (player org).** Recruit friends, share a clubhouse/HQ, pool
  funds, divide roles (driver, hacker, gunner, face — echoing the leads).
- **Co-op heists.** The four-phase heist system ([doc 07](07-missions.md)) built
  for real players in each role. Communication and trust *are* the mechanic.
- **Run businesses.** Own and operate fronts, smuggling routes, and chop shops
  that generate income while you're offline (capped, fair).
- **Freelance hustles.** Drop-in jobs for solo or small groups; always something
  to do in 15 minutes.
- **Living-world events.** Dynamic county-wide events (a storm, a port shipment,
  a faction war flashpoint) that pull players together — cooperative or
  competitive by design, never forced griefing.

## PvP — consent-forward

- **Opt-in conflict.** Open-world PvP is structured (contested zones, declared
  crew wars, event arenas), not a constant gank-fest. Players who want peace can
  largely find it; players who want war have arenas for it.
- **Anti-grief systems:** passive modes, bad-sport handling, matchmaking by
  intent, and no reward for spawn-camping randoms.
- **Skill expression, not gear walls.** Outcomes hinge on play, not on who spent
  more.

## Monetization charter (hard rules)

This is the part most live games get wrong. Our charter:

1. **No pay-to-win.** Nothing purchasable grants a gameplay-power advantage. Money
   and progression come from *playing*.
2. **No randomized paid loot boxes / gambling-for-power.**
3. **Cosmetics & convenience only**, and even convenience never bypasses skill or
   sells safety from other players.
4. **Earnable = the default.** Anything buyable is also reasonably earnable in-game.
5. **No artificial grind inflation** to pressure purchases. Economy tuned for the
   non-paying player first.
6. **Expansions add content, not paywalls** to existing systems.

If a monetization idea fails any rule above, it's dead. The business model is
"a world worth living in," not "a slot machine."

## Seasons & live content

- **Seasonal updates** add story chapters, heists, vehicles, businesses, and
  world events — earned through play, themed around the city's life (a festival,
  a storm season, an election, a development boom).
- **Narrative continuity:** online stays canon-adjacent to the city's fiction
  (the flood keeps rising, factions keep warring), so the world *evolves* instead
  of resetting.

## Technical & social guardrails

- **Small instanced sessions** for stability and social cohesion (you know the
  people you're playing with), federated into a larger persistent economy.
- **Robust moderation & reporting**, voice/text filtering options, and strong
  parental/accessibility controls.
- **Cross-progression** where platform-feasible; **fair-play / anti-cheat** as a
  first-class system.

## Relationship to single-player

- Shared systems and content pipeline, **separate balance and economy** so neither
  warps the other.
- Story-spoiler-safe: online avoids spoiling the campaign's endings.
- **The campaign is never gated behind online,** and online is never required to
  "complete" the game.
