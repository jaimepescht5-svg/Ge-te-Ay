# 06 — Map & Districts

## Geography at a glance

Solano County is an archipelago-and-mainland sprawl: a dense island city
(**Port Soleil**) linked by **causeways** to barrier islands, a working port, an
inland suburb, and a vast swamp interior. The design goal is **contrast on a
short drive** — towers to shacks to swamp in minutes — and **water everywhere**
as both barrier and highway.

```
                 N
        [ Cayo Brava / The Keys ]
                 |  (causeway)
   [ The Reach ]-+-[ DOWNTOWN CORE ]-[ Marisol Heights ]
   (industrial)  |        |               (wealth, hills)
                 |   [ The Cut ]
                 | (canals, old town)
        [ Bayou Verde / The Glades ]----[ Sabal Springs ]
              (swamp interior)            (inland suburb)
                 S
```

## The districts

### 1. Downtown Core — *"The Strip"*
Glass towers, deco hotels, neon, clubs, and the financial district. By day:
suits and tourists. By night: the city's id. Highest density, highest security,
highest stakes. Home to Crane's flagship tower and the Vega-owned casinos.
- **Feel:** Vertical, dazzling, dangerous. Heists with rooftop and crowd play.
- **Flood role:** storm-surge floods the low avenues; the towers stay lit on
  generators — a literal high/low-ground divide.

### 2. The Cut — *"Old Soleil"*
The historic canal district: pastel rowhouses, supper clubs, street markets, a
working-class melting pot being "revitalized" out of existence. The crew's
emotional home turf; Tomás's garage is here.
- **Feel:** Warm, lived-in, intimate. Foot chases through alleys and markets.
- **Flood role:** canals overtop first; boat traversal becomes essential.

### 3. Marisol Heights — *"The Heights"*
The only real high ground: old-money mansions, gated estates, country clubs,
private security. Where the people buying the city actually live.
- **Feel:** Quiet, manicured, hostile to outsiders. Stealth-heavy infiltration.
- **Flood role:** stays dry — which is the whole point of who lives there.

### 4. The Reach — *"Hialto Industrial"*
The container port, rail yards, refineries, and warehouses. Smuggling, scrap,
and the Vega family's logistical heart. Sprawling, low, and full of cover.
- **Feel:** Gritty, mechanical, dark. Vehicle and crane set-pieces; gunfights.
- **Flood role:** toxic flood risk; storm chaos at the docks.

### 5. Cayo Brava & the Keys
Barrier islands reached by a long, gorgeous causeway: beaches, marinas,
party-houses, hidden coves, and Coast Guard blind spots. Smuggling by sea.
- **Feel:** Open, bright, fast. Boats, jet-craft, and beach-road driving theater.
- **Flood role:** first to evacuate; eerie and empty as the storm nears.

### 6. Bayou Verde / The Glades
The vast swamp interior: airboats, fishing camps, hidden hideouts, an
alligator-farm front, off-grid militias, and Mama Yolette's supper-club network.
- **Feel:** Wild, lawless, beautiful and threatening. Airboat chases, ambushes.
- **Flood role:** tide reshapes the channels constantly; the most dynamic terrain.

### 7. Sabal Springs
The faded inland suburb: strip malls, motels, megachurches, a dead mall, trailer
parks, and the county's working poor. The "real America" the postcards skip.
- **Feel:** Sun-blasted, melancholy, satirical. Sprawling roads; suburban scores.
- **Flood role:** ironically safer ground — and where the displaced end up.

## Connective tissue (traversal theater)

- **The Causeways** — multi-mile bridges over open water; the iconic chase
  arenas. One drawbridge is a recurring set-piece.
- **The Metro & the El** — a partial rail line for fast-travel and rooftop
  chases.
- **The Canals** — Old Soleil's waterways; a parallel boat-road network.
- **The Interstate loop** — the high-speed ring road; pursuit territory.

## Design principles for the map

- **No filler tiles.** Every block has a reason to stop — a hustle, a shop, a
  character, a vista, a secret.
- **Verticality where it counts** (Downtown, the Heights) and **horizontality for
  speed** (causeways, Sabal, the Glades).
- **Landmarks for navigation** — the Crane tower, the drawbridge, the lighthouse
  on Cayo Brava, the dead mall — so players map the city in their heads.
- **The flood line is on the map.** Players can *see* who's safe and who isn't,
  which is the game's thesis rendered as level geometry.

## Scale & density intent

Big enough to feel like a real county and to make a great car matter, but
**curated, not bloated** — density and incident over raw square mileage. The
drive from the Glades to Downtown should feel like crossing a whole world, and
should never be boring.
