# 07 — Missions & Heists

## Mission design philosophy

- **Authored seeds, emergent flowers.** A mission sets up a situation and a goal;
  the systems (Heat, traffic, weather, AI) make every run different.
- **Approach freedom is real.** Loud, quiet, and clever are mechanically and
  economically distinct — not flavor text on a linear corridor.
- **Fail-forward.** A blown plan should pivot into a *better story*, not a reload
  screen. Failure states are last resorts.
- **Switching is the verb.** The best missions are choreographed for the Crew
  Wheel — three jobs happening at once.

## Mission types

| Type | What it is | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Story missions** | The main campaign beats | Backbone |
| **Heists / Scores** | Multi-phase, planned set-pieces (the marquee) | ~8–10 major |
| **Crew jobs** | Mid-size jobs that build money & reputation | Many |
| **Hustles** | Repeatable freelance work (courier, repo, collections) | Infinite |
| **Side stories** | Character-driven arcs for NPCs & the leads | Dozens |
| **Emergent encounters** | Systemic, unscripted incidents | Constant |

## The Heist System (the marquee feature)

Heists are the game's signature, structured in four phases:

### Phase 1 — Setup (Intel)
- A score becomes available via a contact or discovery.
- **Scout** the location yourself (Theo's *Overwatch* shines here): tag guards,
  cameras, exits, vault types, patrol routes. Intel persists.
- Buy intel, blueprints, or insider help to skip legwork — for a cut.

### Phase 2 — Planning (Approach)
- Choose an **approach archetype**, each opening different prep:
  - **Quiet** — stealth, disguises, hacking; max payout, min Heat, high skill.
  - **Loud** — force, firepower, speed; reliable but expensive and hot.
  - **Clever** — a trick: a con, a swap, an inside man, a disaster (use the storm).
- Choose **crew & gear:** assign leads, hire specialist NPC crew (driver, hacker,
  gunner) at a cost/skill tradeoff, buy equipment.
- **Tradeoffs are economic:** cheaper crew = more risk; bigger guns = more Heat;
  better intel = bigger cut to the source. The plan is a budget.

### Phase 3 — Execution
- Multi-stage, **multi-character** play: switch between leads handling parallel
  tasks (Rae staging the escape, Theo on cameras, Frankie on the floor).
- **Live improvisation:** the plan *will* deviate. Alarms, bad luck, a panicked
  civilian, a sudden tide — the systems push back and reward adaptation.
- **Optional objectives** (extra vaults, intel, a personal grudge) for greed-vs-
  caution tension.

### Phase 4 — Escape & Aftermath
- Beat the **Heat** ([doc 09](09-weapons-combat.md)): lose pursuit, ditch the
  vehicle, change clothes, reach a safehouse.
- **Split & launder** the take ([doc 10](10-economy-progression.md)); pay the
  crew; bank the rest. Greed in execution = more to lose in escape.
- **Consequences ripple:** factions react, Heat lingers in that district, Boyd
  gets a new lead, and the world remembers.

## Sample heist — "The Ark Gala"
A charity gala aboard Crane's flagship arcology, the night before landfall.
- **Quiet:** infiltrate as catering staff (Frankie's con + Theo's access),
  crack the private vault during the auction.
- **Loud:** crash the causeway approach, take the gala hostage, blast the vault.
- **Clever:** trigger an early evacuation (fake the storm alert) and rob the
  place in the panic as everyone flees — the storm is your accomplice.
Each path has distinct prep, distinct crew needs, distinct escapes (helipad,
sea, or service tunnels), and distinct fallout.

## Side stories (anchors → see [doc 04](04-story.md))

- **The Reclamation** (Crane's machine), **Storm Chasers** (disaster-looters),
  **The Gospel of Gain** (megachurch laundering), **Open Water** (Keys
  smuggling). Each is a 4–6 mission arc with its own characters, mechanics flavor,
  and rewards (a business, a vehicle, a crew specialist, an ending variable).

## Mission quality bar

Every story/heist mission must pass:
1. **A new idea** (mechanic, location, or character beat) the player hasn't seen.
2. **A choice** that the systems or story actually register.
3. **A retellable moment** — the thing the player describes to a friend.
4. **A clean read** — the player always knows the goal and why they failed.
