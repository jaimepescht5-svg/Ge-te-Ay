# 26 — Pitch & Positioning

> The one-page elevator pitch and the market framing — how NEON DELTA is
> described in a room, on a store page, and against the genre. Built to sell the
> game *and* to keep it honest to the pillars ([doc 01](01-vision.md)).

---

## The elevator pitch (30 seconds)

> **NEON DELTA** is an open-world crime saga set in **Port Soleil** — a gorgeous,
> corrupt, sinking coastal city that's one hurricane away from the bottom of the
> sea. Play **three switchable outlaws** — a getaway driver, a burned hacker, and
> a motor-mouthed grifter — bound by one botched score and hunted by a cartel, a
> developer-billionaire, and a crooked cop. Drive, scheme, shoot, and pull
> **planned heists your way** across a living county of neon strips, swamp, and
> open water, while the tide rises and a storm named Odette closes in. It's the
> American hustle, told in salt and neon — **grab your piece before the lights go
> out.**

## The hook (one line)

**A sun-soaked crime sandbox about three desperate people trying to out-hustle a
sinking city — and the people who own it — before the storm takes everything.**

## The three things we sell first

1. **The switch** — three lives, one story; swap between leads anytime, mid-heist
   or mid-chase. The signature verb ([doc 05](05-gameplay-mechanics.md)).
2. **Heists your way** — Quiet / Loud / Clever, planned and improvised, where
   botching the plan makes the best stories ([doc 07](07-missions.md)).
3. **A city that's alive and drowning** — tide, storms, factions, and a flood line
   you can see on the map; a world with a thesis ([doc 19](19-living-world-ai.md)).

## What makes it different (positioning)

| The genre usually... | NEON DELTA instead... |
|---|---|
| Treats the map as a static stage | Makes tide/storm/flood a **live gameplay system** |
| Offers loud-vs-stealth as flavor | Makes Quiet/Loud/**Clever** mechanically + economically real |
| Uses switchable leads as a gimmick | Builds the **whole story & heist play** around the switch |
| Monetizes online with FOMO & pay-to-win | Ships a **public anti-predatory charter** ([doc 11](11-online-multiplayer.md)) |
| Is nihilistic for edge | Is **sincere pulp** — mocks institutions, loves people ([doc 01](01-vision.md)) |
| Bolts on accessibility later | Treats accessibility as a **launch pillar** ([doc 23](23-accessibility.md)) |

## Audience

- **Primary:** open-world crime/action fans who want a story-driven sandbox with
  freedom *and* heart; players who love heist fantasy and emergent-story bragging.
- **Secondary:** narrative/character-driven players (the three leads),
  driving/car-culture fans, and systems-sandbox players (the living city).
- **Welcomed by design:** players who bounce off twitch-difficulty or
  pay-to-win — granular difficulty + a fair economy let them in.

## Tone & rating intent

- **Mature (adult) rating** — crime, violence, satire, strong language — but
  **edge from character and stakes, not cruelty or shock**
  ([doc 09](09-weapons-combat.md)). Content-sensitivity controls included
  ([doc 23](23-accessibility.md)).

## Why now

- The themes — climate pressure, who-owns-the-city gentrification, catastrophe-as-
  content, gig-economy precarity — are **the air we breathe**, and the crime
  sandbox is the perfect satirical lens for them. A genre everyone loves, pointed
  at the moment we're living in.

## The store-page blurb (consumer voice)

> *Welcome to Port Soleil: paradise built on a sandbar, beautiful, doomed, and
> for sale. Three strangers. One botched score. A cartel, a billionaire, and a
> bent cop who all want them dead. Drive the causeways at golden hour, pull the
> perfect heist, launder it through a car wash, and watch the tide rise on
> everyone who thought they were high and dry. Switch between three outlaws to
> beat jobs your way — loud, quiet, or clever — in a living, sinking city where
> the smartest play is rarely the loudest. A storm's coming. Get yours before it
> lands.*

## Elevator-pitch guardrails

- **Lead with the city and the hook,** not a feature list.
- **Never compare by naming a competitor** — define ourselves by our *own* pillars
  ([doc 12](12-legal-distinctiveness.md)).
- **Promise only what the pillars guarantee** — the pitch is a contract with the
  player, especially on monetization and accessibility.
