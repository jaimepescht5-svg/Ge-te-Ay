# 09 — Weapons, Combat & Heat

## Philosophy

Combat is **a tool, not the only tool.** Many situations can be solved by
driving, sneaking, talking, or cleverness. When violence happens, it should feel
**weighty, readable, and consequential** — not a power fantasy that erases the
story's stakes. The smartest play is usually *not* the loudest.

## Gunplay

- **Third-person cover shooter** with snappy, optional cover-snap, blind-fire,
  lean, and a quick free-aim option for experts.
- **Weighted aiming:** weapons have real recoil, sway, and handling personality;
  aim assist is fully scalable for accessibility.
- **Hit reactions & damage:** locational damage on enemies; readable enemy states
  (suppressed, wounded, panicked, surrendering). Civilians and surrender states
  exist so the player can choose restraint.
- **Gore tuned, not gratuitous:** impactful but not torture-porn; a content slider
  softens it. Edge comes from tension, not viscera.

| Class | Examples | Niche |
|---|---|---|
| **Melee** | bat, wrench, knife, machete | Quiet, desperate, characterful |
| **Sidearms** | pistols, revolvers, machine pistols | Concealable, low Heat |
| **SMGs** | compact автоs | CQB, drive-bys |
| **Shotguns** | pump, auto, breaching | Rooms and doors |

## Concealment & legality

- **Concealed vs. open carry** matters: walk into a bank with a rifle and you're
  already in trouble; a concealed pistol draws no attention until drawn.
- **The right tool lowers Heat:** suppressed and non-lethal options are a whole
  playstyle, not an afterthought.

## Melee & takedowns

- Grounded, **timing-and-positioning** melee (block, dodge, grapple, environment
  takedowns) — not a deep fighting game, but satisfying and brutal-quick.
- **Stealth takedowns** (lethal and non-lethal) are core to quiet approaches;
  body discovery raises alarm (NPC memory).
- **Frankie's *Hot Streak*** empowers melee chains and finishers.

## Stealth

- **Detection model:** sight (cones, light, distance), sound (footsteps,
  gunfire, alarms), and **investigation states** (curious → searching → alert →
  pursuit) with believable de-escalation if you break contact.
- **Tools:** disguises, distractions, hacked cameras, tagged enemies (Theo),
  silenced takedowns, and the environment (lights, water, crowds).
- **Pacifist viability:** a full quiet, no-kill run of most missions is a
  supported, rewarded playstyle.

## The Heat System (wanted/pursuit)

A **legible, escalating** response model replacing any "random cop swarm":

### Heat tiers
1. **Noticed** — a witness or camera clocks you; reportable but not yet reported.
2. **Reported** — a call goes out; patrols converge on your *last known
   position*, not your literal location.
3. **Pursuit** — active chase; spike strips, roadblocks, more units.
4. **Crackdown** — air support, SWAT-equivalent, district lockdown.

### Losing Heat (player agency, not luck)
- **Break line of sight** and stay out of the search cone to drop tiers.
- **Last-known-position** logic: cops search where they *think* you are — juke
  them and the cordon misplaces itself.
- **Change your signature:** swap vehicles, respray, change clothes, ditch a hot
  weapon, blend into a crowd.
- **Safehouses & cooldowns:** reach cover and lie low; corruption/bribes can cut
  Heat at a cost.

### Fairness rules
- **No teleporting cops.** Pursuit units have believable origins and travel time.
- **Always a read:** the HUD shows *why* you're wanted and *where* they think you
  are, so escape is a skill, not a dice roll.
- **Heat lingers locally:** a district stays hot for a while after a big job —
  pushing players to spread their crimes around the county.

## Faction "Heat" beyond the law

The Vegas, Crane's private security, and street crews run **parallel pursuit
systems** with their own triggers and territories — sometimes the scariest
people chasing you don't wear badges.
