# 04 — Story & Structure

> Spoiler warning: this is the full narrative skeleton.

## Themes

- **Reinvention vs. the past.** Everyone in Port Soleil is fleeing a former self.
  The city is too — paving over what it was. Can you become someone new, or just
  better at being who you were?
- **Who really owns a place.** The people who build a city vs. the people who buy
  it. Crime as the honest version of capitalism.
- **The flood as reckoning.** You can't out-hustle the tide. What do you save
  when you can't save everything?

## Structure overview

A **three-act**, ~30–40 hour main campaign with a branching final act and three
distinct endings, plus a deep web of side stories. Told through three switchable
leads whose paths converge, diverge, and converge again.

---

### Prologue — "The Sure Thing"
Frankie ropes Rae and Theo into a "clean" warehouse score that turns out to be a
front for **Vega** cartel cash *and* a stash Sterling Crane's people were about
to seize. It goes catastrophically loud. They escape with a bag of money that
belongs to two factions who now both want them dead, and they're caught on a
camera that Det. Boyd will spend the game chasing. The three are now welded
together. **Tutorializes** driving (Rae), stealth/hacking (Theo), and combat
(Frankie), then a three-way switchable escape.

---

### Act I — "Small Fish"
Broke, hunted, and useless apart, the crew builds a foundation: a base (Tomás's
garage), a fence, first allies (Mama Yolette in the Glades), and a reputation.
They take low-level scores to survive while learning the board — discovering that
the Vegas and Crane are at *war* over the coast, and a smart crew could get rich
in the crossfire. **Ends** when they pull their first real heist and pick a side
they'll regret.

### Act II — "Crossfire"
The crew plays both sides, growing in money, crew, and capability. Each lead's
personal story deepens: Rae's garage and father, Theo's blacklist and sister,
Frankie's spiraling debts and old crew. The double game gets harder; loyalties
fray. **Midpoint gut-punch:** a betrayal (rotatable in design — Pelham flips, or
a crew ally is killed, or Boyd makes an arrest) that forces them to stop playing
sides and start playing for themselves. **Ends** with the discovery of Crane's
real plan: he *wants* the hurricane to hit, has the data to profit from it, and
has the city's evacuation/relief money earmarked for The Ark.

### Act III — "Landfall"
A hurricane named in-fiction (e.g., **Hurricane Odette**) bears down. The city
empties; the law thins; the gloves come off. The crew plans one last,
career-defining score — to rob Crane and/or the Vegas blind during the storm,
when no one's watching and everyone's drowning. The act is a **branching
crescendo** built around the storm set-piece.

---

## Branching & endings

Choices across the game (which faction you favored, who you spared, what you did
with the money, who you kept loyal) feed a final fork. Three core endings, each
with variants:

1. **"Clean Break"** — The crew gets out with the money and their lives, scattering
   before the water rises. Bittersweet: they're free, but Port Soleil is lost.
2. **"Scorched Earth"** — They burn it all down — Crane, the Vegas, the cops —
   taking the powerful with them at terrible cost. Pyrrhic and operatic.
3. **"High Ground"** — They turn the last score outward: expose Crane, redirect
   the relief money, and stay to rebuild — choosing the city over the getaway.
   Hopeful, hard-won, and the "true" thematic ending.

Each lead also has a **personal coda** that resolves independently of the main
fork, based on their own arc choices (does Rae save the garage? does Theo get Ada
clear? does Frankie finally walk away a winner — or die a hero?).

## Pacing & switching as narrative device

- **Tension switching:** scripted moments force a switch at the worst time (one
  lead pinned, switch to the other to save them) — mechanizing "we only survive
  together."
- **Perspective reveals:** the same event seen from two leads recontextualizes a
  betrayal. We trust who we play.
- **Downtime vignettes:** switching to an idle lead can trigger a personal beat,
  keeping all three present even when the plot follows one.

## Side narratives (anchors)

- **"The Reclamation"** — following Crane's development machine block by block;
  the slow, legal crime under the illegal one.
- **"Storm Chasers"** — a crew of disaster-looters and a streamer documenting the
  city's death; satire of catastrophe-as-content.
- **"The Gospel of Gain"** — a prosperity megachurch laundering for the Vegas;
  Frankie's old flame is in deep.
- **"Open Water"** — Cayo Brava smuggling and the people the Coast Guard forgot.
